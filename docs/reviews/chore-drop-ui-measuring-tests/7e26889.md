## Findings (most severe first)

1. **[major, not introduced here; needs his call before merge]** Some kept harness tests wait
   on the real clock. That breaks his condition, verbatim: "anything else timing-based must use
   fake timers, never the real clock". The PR body hands this check to the review ("The review
   checks every other kept timing test against your condition"), and it fails here:
   - `apps/web/test/harness/discovery-gesture.harness.ts:74` and `:121`: `page.waitForTimeout(600)`
     before asserting the deck did *not* advance;
   - `apps/web/test/harness/discovery-reveal.harness.ts:260`: the same 600ms wait before a
     "did not advance" assertion;
   - `apps/web/test/harness/harness.ts:146,149`: `dragHorizontally` spaces its pointer moves
     with `waitForTimeout`;
   - `apps/web/test/harness/kabinet-animal-form.harness.ts:201`: a 1s real `setTimeout` delay
     injected into a route.

   Failure scenario: on a slow runner the spring is still moving at 600ms and the negative
   assertion reads a stale state, so it passes whether or not the gesture was broken. The tests
   prove less than they claim.

   Fix: Playwright's `page.clock` (install, then `runFor`), or poll for the at-rest state before
   asserting. The builder can do this in this PR, or Oleksii can agree to a follow-up row. Either
   way it should be settled before merge, because merging records his condition as met.

   Also on the real clock, though these are microtask flushes rather than timing tests:
   `useReveal.test.tsx:180,212,243,272` (`setTimeout(r, 10)`) and the `setTimeout(r, 0)` calls in
   `SwipeDeck.test.tsx` and `use-feed-deck.test.tsx`. The 280ms test and the reduced-motion tests
   (UI-U3, UI-U4) do meet the condition: their describe blocks run under `vi.useFakeTimers()`
   (`use-swipe-gesture.test.tsx:156`), and so does the velocity block (`:603`).
